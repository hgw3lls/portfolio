02 Artist statement Download PDF

My work operates through fiction—not as representation, but as a force that produces conditions.

I am interested in how narrative, myth, mediated images, and technological systems organize collective desire, and how these structures persist, circulate, and mutate across media environments.

The work develops across film, sound, installation, performance, and computational media—not as movement between disciplines, but as interference between forms. Image, sound, code, and spatial systems interact to produce unstable relationships between perception, atmosphere, and interpretation. Rather than constructing fixed narratives, these works build environments that audiences enter, navigate, and activate through participation and duration.

I return repeatedly to temporal displacement. Fragments of possible worlds appear out of sequence—past events reconfigured as future projections, speculative structures embedded within existing archives, signals that arrive too early or too late. These works accumulate through repetition, drift, and recursion, allowing fiction to operate not as storytelling alone, but as an active condition shaping perception and experience.

Installations and audiovisual environments often unfold through projected image, fragmented archives, distorted voice, live transmission, feedback systems, and accumulated audiovisual residue. Sound functions as an affective force before interpretation stabilizes, producing atmospheres of instability, delay, and pressure.

Computational systems extend this condition. I engage generative and networked media not simply as tools for production, but as infrastructures that compress, redistribute, and reorganize perception. Works such as Artificial Cinema, N2: The Secretary, HYPNAGNOSIS, and TRANSMISSIONS operate as layered configurations of language, signal, sound, and image—systems that can be entered, but never fully resolved.

Archives—both real and fictional—are treated as material. Documents, recordings, found media, and synthetic images are recombined into unstable configurations that blur distinctions between memory, projection, simulation, and historical trace.

There is no stable image at the end of this process—only signals that continue to circulate.
